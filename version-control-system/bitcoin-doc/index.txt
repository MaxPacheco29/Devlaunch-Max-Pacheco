What is bitcoin?

Bitcoin is a decentralized digital currency that operates without the need for a central authority,
such as a bank or government. It was introduced in 2009 by an anonymous person or group using the pseudonym Satoshi
Nakamoto. Bitcoin transactions are recorded on a public ledger called the blockchain, ensuring transparency and
security. Unlike traditional currencies, Bitcoin is based on a peer-to-peer network, allowing users to send and
receive funds globally with lower fees and greater privacy. Due to its limited supply of 21 million coins, 
Bitcoin is often considered a store of value, similar to digital gold.

How Does Bitcoin Work?
1. Blockchain Technology
Bitcoin transactions are recorded on a public, distributed ledger called the blockchain. The blockchain consists of blocks of transactions that are securely linked together in chronological order. Each block contains:

A list of validated Bitcoin transactions
A cryptographic reference (hash) to the previous block
A unique mathematical puzzle that must be solved through mining
Once a block is confirmed, it is added to the blockchain, making it immutable and secure from tampering.

2. Decentralization and Peer-to-Peer Transactions
Unlike traditional financial systems that rely on centralized entities like banks, Bitcoin operates on a decentralized network of computers (nodes) spread across the world.

Transactions are peer-to-peer (P2P), meaning they occur directly between users without intermediaries.
Anyone with an internet connection can send or receive Bitcoin, regardless of location or financial status.
3. Bitcoin Mining: Securing the Network
Bitcoin mining is the process of verifying transactions and adding them to the blockchain. Miners use powerful computers to solve complex mathematical problems, known as proof-of-work (PoW).

The first miner to solve the problem gets to add the next block to the blockchain and is rewarded with newly minted Bitcoin and transaction fees.
This process helps secure the network and ensures that transactions remain valid.
4. Limited Supply and Scarcity
One of Bitcoin’s most significant features is its finite supply. Unlike fiat currencies, which can be printed indefinitely, Bitcoin has a maximum supply of 21 million coins. This scarcity contributes to its value and makes it resistant to inflation.

5. Security and Transparency
Bitcoin transactions are recorded on the blockchain, making them publicly viewable and transparent. However, Bitcoin addresses are pseudonymous, meaning they do not directly reveal the identity of users.

Transactions are cryptographically secure, making Bitcoin highly resistant to fraud and counterfeiting.

Bitcoin has transformed the financial world by introducing a decentralized, secure, and borderless digital 
currency. With its limited supply, transparency, and resistance to censorship, 
Bitcoin serves as both a store of value and a medium of exchange. Despite challenges like scalability, 
regulatory concerns, and energy consumption, its adoption continues to grow among individuals, businesses, 
and institutions. As technology and regulations evolve, Bitcoin’s role in the global economy is likely to expand, 
solidifying its position as a revolutionary financial asset.
